Context

Fatigue and distraction monitoring, running on your phone

Mount the phone so the front camera can see your face, then start. The camera feed is processed on this device and never uploaded — there is no server to send it to.

  • Microsleep — eyes closed past 1.5 s
  • Drowsiness — PERCLOS over a rolling minute
  • Yawning — counted when the mouth closes again
  • Distraction — head off the road past 2 s
  • Seatbelt — the strap’s two edges, found by hand rather than by a model
  • Expression — brow and mouth shape, smoothed
  • Camera — a covered lens, and a feed that has stopped moving
  • Safety index — each lapse weighed by how bad it is, how trustworthy the measurement is, and what the situation is doing to the cost of it

The first few seconds calibrate: look straight ahead while it learns your neutral head position, and keep blinking normally while it learns your eye shape. A fixed blink threshold misreads some people’s eyes as permanently shut.

Two more detectors, each with a model of its own to download

Leave both off and the app needs one 3.6 MB model and runs at full speed. Everything in the list above works without them.

Nothing above needs setting. The hour comes from this device’s own clock, the cabin light off the camera, and the forecast from your location the first time you allow it — after that the coordinates are remembered and no drive asks again. Press Get the forecast above to do that part now, before the camera starts, rather than answering a location prompt over a monitor that is already running. The two selectors only exist to override any of it, so the weighting can be demonstrated without waiting for 3 am or for rain. The weather override is what the W key does on the desktop, and the panel always says whether a reading came from you, from the forecast, or from nowhere at all — because a ×1.00 from a clear sky and a ×1.00 from ignorance weigh the same and mean nothing like the same thing.

What the score is doing. Each active condition costs base × severity × confidence × context, and the breakdown above prints all four. Severity is how bad this instance is — a six second glance away against a two second one. Confidence is how much the measurement deserves to be trusted: an eye reading in a dark cabin or on a turned face is worth less than one taken head-on. Context is the only layer that can push a penalty up: the small hours, hours already at the wheel, and a road that leaves less room to recover. Severity and confidence can only ever discount, so base stays the most any one condition can cost and the 100-point budget keeps its meaning.

Ported from the desktop system in this repository. Head pose comes from MediaPipe’s own facial transformation matrix rather than solvePnP, which avoids the mirror ambiguity that made a turned head read as turned the other way. The one detector that is not a faithful port is the expression reading: the desktop runs FER+, a trained classifier, and this reads the face blendshapes that come free with the landmarks instead — cruder, and 35 MB cheaper for the smallest penalty in the budget. It is called an expression here rather than an emotion because that is what is actually being measured.
